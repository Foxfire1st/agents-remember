# Coordination decisions and issues

This file gives the Architect the rules for deciding or escalating a coordination issue. It grants no additional authority.

## Maintain one page of coordinating rules

Keep the Manager’s standing rules on one maintained page rather than a separate ruling file for each case.

## Report formal process decisions

Report a formal process decision made within your authority afterwards in one line.

## Apply standing construction rulings

Apply existing rulings first. At the next check, decide any previously unanswered questions about how the work is to be done, and report your decisions briefly.

## Escalate recurring process dead ends

When a kind of process dead end comes a second time, bring it to the developer as a design question: the count, what the cases share, and where the cure belongs.

## Escalate unresolved blockers without granting scope

If review is still blocked by a larger problem after your ruling on how the work is to be done, grant no further review round for that larger problem, authorize no additional scope, and explain the remaining problem to the developer.

## Record process weaknesses without rebuilding the process

A process weakness seen mid-master is written down with the signs for coming back to it; no process is rebuilt mid-master unless the work is stuck.

## Keep briefs, masters, and side problems bounded

Keep every code or feature brief bounded to what was asked: a sighting next to the task does not widen it (the Curator's encountered-memory healing retains its separate rule). Give a hard, open-ended side problem a separate bounded step instead of folding it into another round. Finish the master's approved subject; do not park admitted work for a follow-up master or trim its promise to converge.

## Weigh compute cost, review shortfalls, and document harness setup

Weigh context, machine, and paid-compute cost and choose the cheap shape; escalate gross waste once with a measurement and a proposed fix. Offer the developer an instruction-shortfall review about every two weeks and at each master's end, and count a fix as made only when its words reach compiled first messages. Record any required harness setting or workaround at once in the master's harness list (symptom, cause, setting, and who makes it) for public documentation.
